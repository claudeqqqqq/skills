They laughed at my shoes for three years. Then, one Monday, I bought the school.
At Blocks City High, I was the poor kid. Same hoodie. Same shoes. The left one had a hole you could fit a pencil through.
Ryan made sure everyone noticed. Every morning, he knocked my books out of my hands. "Maybe buy real shoes before you come here, Harry." Everyone laughed. Nobody helped.
Then they found Milo. Smallest kid in sixth grade. His lunch hit the floor. I knelt down next to him. "It's okay. I've got you." Ryan grinned. "Look. The poor kid found a pet."
Nobody knew what I did after school. Four o'clock, I delivered pizza. Seven, I fixed the neighbors' laptops. And at midnight, on a computer older than me, I built a game. A tycoon game where you build a school, brick by brick.
In March, it had ten players. In June, ten thousand. By September, four million. I didn't buy shoes. I saved every coin.
Then a letter came home. Blocks City High was closing. Sold, to be torn down for a parking lot. And the man buying it was Ryan's dad.
On Monday, a black car stopped at the gates. I stepped out. Same hoodie. Same shoes. Grandpa followed me with a folder, because I'm fourteen and somebody has to sign. The principal stared. "Harry? You're here for the ownership meeting?" Ryan froze. "Ownership what?"
In the auditorium, the principal tapped the mic. "Blocks City High has a new owner. The money came from a game four million kids play." She looked at me. "Harry built it." Ryan's dad never got his parking lot.
Later, Ryan found me by the lockers. "So are you kicking me out?" His books slipped out of his hands. I picked them up. "No. But from today, nobody here eats lunch off the floor."
Then I called Milo on stage. Free lunch and new shoes, for every kid who needs them.
I kept mine, though. The hole still fits a pencil.
