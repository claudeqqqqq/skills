# Story: formula v2 (from research/notes_story.md)

## What the 4-14M "In this world / In this town" shorts do
1. Sentence 1 states the rule in 7-12 words, finished by ~1.2-3.4 s. No greeting.
2. 3-5 concrete examples in rapid succession, one image each (these are the shots).
3. First-person underdog narrator with the "wrong" version of the rule (outsider, 3 cents, slept inside).
4. Inciting incident breaks/tests the rule. 5. Escalation x2-3 (house -> street -> town). 6. Twist: the rule had a hidden
   purpose or the underdog's flaw is the strength. 7. Short punchline/callback ending, planted earlier.
8. Optional in-world CTA ("Like and subscribe, or it stops smiling").
Length 60-120 s (250-500 words); hits cluster at ~0:45-1:00, ~2:00 and ~3:00. Plant two callbacks in the first 15 s.

## Choosing a premise
Universal, instantly visual rule with a status twist: numbers over heads, a banned normal activity, an inverted safety rule.
AI as setting works when intimate and rule-based, not a generic attack. Generate 8 variants, pick one that (a) has a hook
in <= 2 s, (b) gives the narrator a planted flaw that becomes the key, (c) ends on a punchline.

## Originality / platform risk
Every video needs its own script, cast and sets. YouTube is de-prioritising re-uploads and can demonetise templated
mass-produced videos. Do not copy another creator's plot, characters or footage; reference only structure and pacing.

## Script checks
`tools/pacing.py` (wpm, words in 15 s, shot lengths), read it aloud once, cut every sentence that does not add a picture.
