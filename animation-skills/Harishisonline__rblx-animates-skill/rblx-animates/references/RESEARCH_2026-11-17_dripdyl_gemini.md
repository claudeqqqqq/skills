## Summary: the 10 lessons
1. Sentence 1 = the whole premise, and frame 1 SHOWS the rule working (shrinking boy, numbers over heads, 50 firefighters).
2. Sentence 2 = the cost or condition. Then 3-4 absurd world examples with numbers and authority figures, then "And me?".
3. "Until one night..." brings a family/crush stake; the rule (or the narrator's weakness) is the only way to solve it.
4. On-screen counters / numbered escalation (212 -> 0, Night 1-5, Strike 1-3, a clock) act as a progress bar.
5. Pace: measured 3.7-4.9 words per second (dripdyl 3.7-4.2). Our Alex is ~3.3. Gemini's own pace estimates were wrong.
6. Cuts every 0.5-2.5 s, camera always moving (eased push-ins, whip pans), stepped 2D face swaps on the beat, anime overlays.
7. Smoothness = eased keys (no linear), anticipation, snap-and-hold on impact poses, motion blur only on fast moves.
8. Music changes mood with the story (playful -> tense -> warm/triumphant), silence for a wait, SFX on nearly every cut.
9. Endings: 3-8-word callback gag (dripdyl), emotional truth line, rule-reversal twist, or Part-2 cliffhanger. Mostly no CTA.
10. Two runtime clusters: rule stories 37-57 s; storyline/game stories 135-180 s. Small channels (under 3k subs) still get
    16-28k views on day 1-10 with this format; itsRedBear (35.7k subs) got 8.5M in 3 days.
