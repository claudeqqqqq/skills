# Why Roblox story Shorts go viral: 30 channels + 2 benchmarks (research, 2026-11-17)

Method: 3 Opus research agents, yt-dlp metadata + English auto-captions only (no media downloaded), YouTube Shorts.
Raw data: Mac ~/RobloxStories_tmp/research/{r1,r2,r3,bb}; full reports r1.md (In-this-town, 10 ch + @dripdyl),
r2.md (storyline giants, 10 ch), r3.md (small/new breakout channels, 10 ch). Every number comes from yt-dlp with the video ID.
Not measurable: thumbnails, retention curves, traffic sources, music identity (0 of 112 videos showed a music card).

## Our baseline (Windsor, last 30 days)
IG reels: reach 1.1-1.4k, views 1.4-2.2k, avg watch 34-46 s on 95-110 s videos (~40 %), first-3-s skip 18-33 %, shares 0
(EP3: 20), comments 0-1. YouTube Shorts: 28-1,426 views, avg view 24-45 s. Posting ~1 video per 1-2 days.

## The rules that separate winners from us (counts from the reports)
1. **Premise = personal stake + clock, not a world tour.** Small-channel breakouts (r3, 26 at >=10x subs) put the narrator or
   family in danger/deadline by ~6 s or have a clear antagonist. Whimsical no-threat rules lose (Acorns Are Money 0.7x,
   The Moon Is an Egg 0.2x). 17 of 27 In-this-town hits have family/best-friend stakes; ~15 of 27 use a visible counter/countdown.
2. **First sentence = the rule** (25 of 27): "In this town/world, [rule with a number]" then often "You see, ..." (11 of 27).
3. **Gag montage first**: 3-6 funny side effects of the rule in 5-30 s, then "Me? I'm..." at ~15 s, crisis at 35-55 s.
4. **Fast and dense**: leaders ~4.2 caption words/s (~250 wpm). We run ~200 wpm with pauses.
5. **Runtime**: giants now 121-174 s (median 143; dripdyl 136-173). Small-channel breakouts median ~59 s (17 of 26 <= 90 s);
   long breakouts (140-165 s) all had a countdown stake. => short (60-75 s) to break out, long only for proven premises.
6. **Titles**: 2-6 words + 1 emoji, the rule or "I [did X]" or "The [Noun]". Titles starting "In This Town" top out at
   0.36-3.6M; the 4 biggest rule breakouts drop the prefix ("The Invisible Red String" 19.4M, "Every Home Has A Monster Alarm" 16.7M).
7. **Endings**: 0 of 32 transcribed giant videos end on a viewer question; they end on a 1-8-word callback. 7 of 27 add a
   like/subscribe line framed as an in-story threat (smaller channels). Comments come from a two-option question asked
   BEFORE the reveal ("Comment Chloe or billionaire" -> 3,900 comments, 8x its sibling) or from a debatable twist.
8. **Volume**: leaders post 11-41 Shorts/week; small breakout channels 7-26/week; breakouts land ~1 in 10-40 uploads.
9. **Wordless/dialogue Shorts (13-59 s) get the most raw views; narrated stories get 2-4x the like rate and series loyalty.**
10. **Series**: numbered parts held 4-6M each (Noobified BEAST Pt.1-6); recurring named narrator/cast in 4 of 10 small channels.

## Winning premise families (views)
night monster / one survival rule (33.8M, 31.1M); playground game turned huge or lethal (Marco Polo 25.5M, Shadow Tag 19.3M,
Floor Is Lava 14.2M); reversed world (Underwater 24.4M, Alphabet Is Alive 24.3M); limited-count rules (Sorry 10 Times 16.8M,
1,000 Jumps 11.0M); love/friendship bound by a rule with a deadline (Red String 19.4M, Eye Contact = Marriage 6.4M);
monster/secret parent with a protective twist (Monster Alarm 16.7M); petty injustice + outsmart (He Stole Everything 3.4M).

## What this means for RBLX ANIMATES (proposal, needs the user's OK)
- Make In-this-town Shorts 60-75 s first (cheaper, faster, matches small-channel breakouts), dense pace (~230-250 wpm).
- Premises from the winning families, with the narrator's family at risk and a number/clock in sentence 1.
- Titles: 2-5 words + emoji, no "In This Town" prefix.
- Ending: callback line; two-option comment question BEFORE the reveal; like/follow only as a short in-story line (or none).
  This changes ENDING_RULE.md and needs the user's decision.
- Volume is the lever we can't match with 2.5-h renders: plan 1-2 Shorts/day with a lighter shot plan (fewer, reusable sets).
